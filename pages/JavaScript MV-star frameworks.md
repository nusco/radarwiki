## Current state (late 2026)

In 2013, single-page apps were new, and dozens of frameworks competed to bring patterns like MVC (Model-View-Controller) and MVVM (Model-View-ViewModel) to the browser. "MV*" was the catch-all name. Of the three frameworks in the blip, AngularJS was the big winner, until Google replaced it with a rewrite called Angular and ended its support in January 2022. Knockout and Ember are still around, but they're niche. React, released in 2013, moved the field away from MVC and toward components that render from state, and today's frameworks (React, Vue, Svelte, Angular) all follow that model. The blip's prediction of "continued churn" came true.

It's long, so read only "What Is JavaScript MV*, Or Rather MV*?", "When Do You Need A JavaScript MV* Framework?" and "The Challenge Of Choice".
- [ ] **~5 min** · Skim: [TodoMVC](https://todomvc.com/) (Addy Osmani, Sindre Sorhus and others) — the project born from that article: the same to-do app in many frameworks. Open the source of an old one, such as Backbone.js or KnockoutJS, next to a modern one, such as React or Svelte, and compare how they connect data to the page.
- [ ] **~5 min** · Read: [AngularJS Officially Reached End of Life](https://www.infoq.com/news/2022/01/angularjs-lts-end/) (Sergio De Simone, InfoQ, Jan 2022, ~1 min) — how the most popular of these frameworks ended.
