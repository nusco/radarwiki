## Current state (late 2026)

The phrase spread in August 2026, after a short blog post by Niklas Gruhn, and KDE quoted it in its draft guidelines on AI ("Don't be a meat proxy"). A meat proxy is a person who forwards an LLM's output to a colleague without reading it, checking it or adding anything. The blip is new in November 2026. It's less a technique than a matter of etiquette, and the Radar includes it because it's a growing problem.

## Level 1 — What it is (~10 min)

Passing on unverified AI output as if it were your own work, so that the person who receives it has to do the checking.

- [ ] **~5 min** · Read: [The Radar blip](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/meat-proxy) (~1 min) — note the comparison with [[Human-in-the-loop theater]]: fake authorship instead of fake approval.
- [ ] **~5 min** · Read: [Don't be a meat proxy](https://gruhn.me/blog/2026-08-03/) (Niklas Gruhn, Aug 2026, ~2 min) — the short post that made the phrase popular, with everyday examples from Slack, code review and chat.

## Level 2 — Check yourself (~10 min)

Look at what you've forwarded lately, and add your own judgment.

- [ ] **~10 min** · **Do:** Look at the last few things you sent that an AI helped you write: messages, review comments, emails. Did you read and check each one, and did you add your own judgment? Rewrite one in your own words, and notice what changed.
