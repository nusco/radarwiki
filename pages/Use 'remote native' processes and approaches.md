## Current state (late 2026)

The blip came out in the first months of the pandemic, when most teams went remote overnight. Since then, many companies have called people back to the office for part or all of the week, but distributed teams are still common, and even a team with one remote member works remotely in practice. The advice in the blip has aged well: default to written, asynchronous communication, and design meetings so that remote people aren't second-class. Some of the tools it names have changed: Google discontinued Jamboard, and GitHub Codespaces became one of several cloud development environments.

## Level 1 — The shapes of remote work (~25 min)

Remote isn't one thing, and each shape of team has its own problems.

- [ ] **~5 min** · Read: [The Radar blip](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/use-remote-native-processes-and-approaches) (~3 min) — read both versions. The first lists practices, like "everybody always remote" meetings. The second lists risks, like burnout from back-to-back video calls.
- [ ] **~20 min** · Read: [Remote versus Co-located Work](https://martinfowler.com/articles/remote-or-co-located.html) (Martin Fowler, Oct 2015, ~6 min for these sections) — linked from the blip. Read "The Many Shades of Remoteness" and "Pay attention to communication patterns". Note why satellite workers have it hardest, and why a remote-first team has to go all in.

## Level 2 — Work async-first (~50 min)

Make writing the default, and meetings the exception.

- [ ] **~25 min** · Read: [A guide to distributed teams](https://increment.com/teams/a-guide-to-distributed-teams/) (Juan Pablo Buriticá and Katie Womersley, Increment, Nov 2019, ~8 min for these sections) — two engineering leaders on running distributed teams at Splice and Buffer. Read "Communication" and "Coordination".
- [ ] **~10 min** · Read: [The async-first manifesto](https://www.asyncagile.org/manifesto) (Sumeet Moghe and contributors, ~2 min) — a short complement to the Agile Manifesto, by a former Thoughtworker. Note the principle that meetings are the last resort, and the one that says trust still needs some time together.
- [ ] **~15 min** · **Do:** List your team's recurring meetings from last week. For each one, decide whether it could be a written update, a shared document, or a shorter meeting. Pick one and propose the change to your team.

## If you have extra time

- [ ] **~45 min** · Read the rest of [A guide to distributed teams](https://increment.com/teams/a-guide-to-distributed-teams/) — on organization, operations and culture, such as onboarding and meeting in person every few months.
